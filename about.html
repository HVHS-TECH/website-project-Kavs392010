About

History

South Korea history features an ancient heritage, a 35-year Japanese colonial period (1910-1945), and a 1948 establishment following WWII division.

  • After the 1950–1953 Korean War, it transformed from a war-torn nation into a major economic, technological, and cultural leader known for rapid industrialization (Miracle on the Han River) and democratization.
  • Ancient & Imperial History: Early kingdoms included Gojoseon (4th century BC), Three Kingdoms (Silla, Baekje, Goguryeo), and the unified Silla Kingdom.
  • Later, Goryeo (918–1392) and the long-lasting Joseon Dynasty (1392–1910) shaped cultural identity, creating Hangeul (the alphabet) and resisting foreign invasions.
  • Japanese Colonization (1910–1945): Japan annexed Korea, dismantling the Joseon Dynasty, causing a significant loss of sovereignty, economic exploitation, and prohibition of Korean culture.
  • Division and War (1945–1953): Following Japan's defeat in 1945, the peninsula was divided at the 38th parallel, with the US in the south and Soviet forces in the north. The Republic of Korea was formally established in 1948, followed by the Korean War (1950–1953), which ended in a stalemate that persists today.

Modern Life & Economy

South Korea is a high-income, technologically advanced nation known for its rapid "Miracle on the Han River" development. It features a strong, export-driven economy focused on electronics, automobiles, and semiconductors. Life is characterized by high education levels, intense competition, and a rapid, urbanized lifestyle, facing challenges from an aging population and low fertility rates.

  • Authoritarianism & Industrialization: Following the war, South Korea was one of the world's poorest nations. A 1961 military coup initiated decades of strict military rule under leaders like Park Chung-hee, focusing on rapid industrialization, which spurred the "Miracle on the Han River".
  • Democratization (1987): The June Democratic Struggle of 1987 overturned military rule, leading to the establishment of the Sixth Republic and a stable, competitive democratic system.
  • Economic & Cultural Global Leader: From the 1990s onward, South Korea developed into a high-tech economy (tech giants like Samsung) and a cultural powerhouse (the "Korean Wave" or Hallyu), becoming a leading exporter of electronics, cars, and pop culture.
  • Current Standing: Today, South Korea is a fully developed nation and a global leader in innovation, while still navigating complex relations with North Korea.

Capital & Cities

South Korea (Republic of Korea) is a mountainous East Asian peninsula nation, boasting a population over 51 million, with high-tech cities, ancient temples, and a robust economy. South Korea’s cities are highly urbanised and connected by efficient public transport, including high-speed trains. Each city contributes to the country’s strong economy, culture, and global influence.

  • Seoul is home to major global companies, advanced technology, and one of the world’s fastest internet networks. The city blends traditional culture (palaces, temples, hanok village) with modern life (skyscrapers, shopping districts and nightlife). It is also the major global hub for k-pop, film, and fashion, influencing trends worldwide.
  • Busan is the country’s largest port and a key centre for trade and tourism, which is famous for its beaches and seafood.
  • Incheon plays an important role in international travel and logistics, with one of the busiest airports in the world.
  • Daegu is known for its manufacturing industries and hot summers.
  • Daejeon is a leading science and research city, often called Korea’s “science hub".
  • Gwangju is known for its cultural heritage and importance in South Korea’s democratic history.
  • Ulsan is a major industrial powerhouse, especially in shipbuilding and automobile production.
  • Sejong was developed as a planned administrative capital to reduce overcrowding in Seoul.
